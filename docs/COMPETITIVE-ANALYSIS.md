# Phase 4 — Competitive Analysis: Household/Family Sharing in Personal Finance Apps

_Prepared for Orange Way, April 2026. Scope: how competitors model partner/household sharing, privacy, advisor access, dependents, encryption, revocation, and recovery. All claims cited._

## 2. Per-app detail

### 2.1 Monarch Money

- **Household model.** Any number of members, all free, all with the same visibility level. Owner/Admin manages billing and can remove members. Members can invite each other's accounts to a shared household space. "Each person will have their own login, but can view each other's data." ([help](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/20926382202004-Monarch-for-Couples-and-Households))
- **Shared Views (2024–25).** Accounts and transactions can be labeled **mine / theirs / ours**. Dashboard, net worth, spending reports, and cash flow can be filtered by owner. By default transactions inherit the account's owner; rules can reassign individual transactions. This is a **filter**, not a privacy control — both partners can still flip the filter and see each other's "mine" data. ([help](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/42228648365076-Shared-Views-in-Monarch))
- **Per-row privacy.** There is a transaction-level "eye / privacy" toggle that marks a transaction private and hides it from the main list. Community docs describe this as the way to conceal "engagement ring / birthday gift" purchases from a partner. Caveat: this is the same "hide transaction" function used to keep transfers out of reports, so it is **not** a cleanly segmented privacy model. ([help](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/4405041904916-Hiding-or-Unhiding-Transactions))
- **Cannot fully hide an account** from a household member; even "hidden" accounts remain visible in Institution Settings or when hidden accounts are expanded. ([help](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/4407859794580-Hiding-an-Account))
- **Advisor.** Dedicated **Professionals Program** at $14.99/mo per active client — advisor has a portal with the client's view, can collaborate, and the client can "choose which accounts you want to share and choose to make the information read-only." ([help](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/11251085750292-Professionals-Program), [pro page](https://www.monarch.com/for-professionals))
- **Revocation.** Member can be removed by owner or by themselves. **Destructive side-effect:** "When a member is removed, any accounts that were added under their login will be removed as well." ([help](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/20926382202004-Monarch-for-Couples-and-Households)) This is a real divorce/breakup hazard — the leaving partner takes their accounts with them and the remaining partner loses that data.
- **Recovery.** Single Admin/Owner. Not E2EE, so Monarch support can restore access via standard identity verification.
- **Bank credentials** flow through Plaid/Finicity/MX; Monarch never stores them ([security](https://www.monarch.com/security)). Each partner's accounts are connected under their own login.

### 2.2 YNAB (YNAB Together)

- **Group model.** Up to 6 people total, one subscription. "YNAB Together allows you to pick and choose the plans you share with individual group members. For instance, Toni can share the 'Household Plan' with her partner Ralph, while keeping that plan private from other group members." ([help](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/ynab-together-B1nS78Cki))
- **Budget-level privacy.** The owner decides per-budget who sees it. Unshared budgets are fully private. This is the **cleanest model in the market** for joint vs. personal separation.
- **Permissions.** Shared budgets grant full edit (accounts, transactions, categories). Only the owner can manage sharing, delete, or Fresh Start a budget.
- **Revocation.** "If a member leaves a group, all budgets they own will remain in their individual user account. The group administrator will lose access to those budgets, and the user will also lose access to all budgets shared with them by other group members." Bank accounts they added to shared budgets become **unlinked**. ([help](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/ynab-together-B1nS78Cki))
- **Dependents.** "Teens invited through YNAB Together will be able to create as many budgets as they like, without seeing any household or personal budgets connected to your account." Kid/Teen/Family templates are published. Minimum age 13 US, 16 outside. ([blog](https://www.ynab.com/blog/set-up-a-teen-budget-in-ynab))
- **Advisor.** No dedicated role — if an advisor joins a group they have the same full-edit access as anyone else.
- **Security.** Industry-standard encryption at rest + in transit. Not zero-knowledge. ([security](https://www.ynab.com/security))
- **Pricing complaint.** Users note repeated price hikes; legacy "grandfathered" rates were eventually sunset. ([Fortune review](https://fortune.com/article/ynab-pros-and-cons/))

### 2.3 Copilot Money

- **Sharing is really shared login.** "Magic Link" emails a temporary-key URL to a second device. "Any additional logged-in devices will have full control of the account." ([help](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/4523792-sharing-your-account-with-a-partner))
- **Couples ($9/mo) / Family ($19/mo) plans** exist (higher-tier billing), but the underlying multi-user model is the same — no separate per-partner views.
- **Per-row privacy.** None. Nothing private from your partner if you've given them your Magic Link.
- **Advisor.** Not a separate role; same Magic Link technique.
- **Dependents.** None.
- **Security.** AES-256 at rest, TLS in transit, Plaid/Finicity for credentials ([security](https://www.copilot.money/privacy-and-security)). Not E2EE.
- **Platform caveat.** iOS/macOS only.

### 2.4 Lunch Money

- **Collaborators per budget**, not per account. Each gets their own login. Pricing is per-budget, not per-person — a big cost advantage for small groups. ([features](https://lunchmoney.app/features/collaboration))
- **Admin vs. collaborator.** Admin owns the subscription and can invite/remove. Both roles have full access to budget data.
- **Privacy.** None within a shared budget; advertised workaround is "each person can have their own personal budget as well as a shared one" but this requires paying per budget.
- **Revocation.** Done from Settings → Collaborators. Docs are thin here; no documented "what happens to data they added" statement.
- **Audit trail.** "You'll be able to see and track who linked which account and who added which transaction." This is one of the better audit trails in the segment. ([features](https://lunchmoney.app/features/collaboration))
- **Dependents, advisor role.** Not first-class.

### 2.5 Rocket Money

- **Primary + Secondary, two people only.** Premium required. Each has their own credentials and MFA. ([help](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/4562634-account-sharing))
- **Asymmetric features.** Credit score is **primary only**. Credit info is never visible to secondary. ([help](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/4562634-account-sharing))
- **Privacy.** None — both see all shared data; "Rocket Money does not currently have a feature that would hide specific accounts between Primary and Secondary users."
- **Revocation is destructive.** "This will reset your Rocket Money account, and you will no longer have access to the primary user's account or data and will have to start fresh with a new Rocket Money account." ([help](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/9184524-removing-a-partner-s-access-to-your-account)) **This is the worst documented revocation UX in the segment.**
- **Advisor, dependent.** Not supported.

### 2.6 Simplifi (Quicken)

- **"Spaces & Sharing"** adds exactly one additional member to a Financial Space. Member needs their own Quicken ID but no separate Simplifi subscription. ([help](https://support.simplifi.quicken.com/en/articles/6443103-how-to-share-your-quicken-simplifi-data))
- **Advisor/accountant.** Supported via same sharing mechanism, so accountants get **full-access** — read-only is the #1 feature request in the community forum and still not shipped as of April 2026 ([community thread](https://community.simplifimoney.com/discussion/5006/limited-access-with-space-sharing-edited)).
- **Privacy.** None.
- **Dependents.** None.

### 2.7 Empower Personal Dashboard (ex-Personal Capital)

- **Joint Owner role.** One primary + one joint per dashboard. Joint can "see and make changes to your dashboard, link, close, and remove accounts. However, a joint owner cannot invite new joint owners." ([help](https://support-personalwealth.empower.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038431573-Can-I-Limit-my-Joint-Owner-s-Access-to-my-Dashboard))
- **Cannot limit** joint owner access — all or nothing.
- **Advisor.** Empower runs its own RIA (advisory agreements are separate). No user-initiated "invite my outside CPA" workflow.
- **Revocation gotcha.** Joint owner of a linked Empower Personal Cash account cannot be removed; the conversion is one-way. ([help](https://support-personalwealth.empower.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038437513-How-do-I-Remove-a-Joint-Owner-From-My-Dashboard))

### 2.8 Actual Budget (open source)

- **Roles.** Basic (create + collaborate) and Admin (plus user directory management and ownership transfer). ([docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/config/multi-user/))
- **Joint workflow.** Docs recommend a shared budget file with both partners as collaborators; per-person separation is achieved by splitting into multiple budget files. ([docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/budgeting/joint-accounts/))
- **E2EE.** Optional. A second password generates an encryption key; the server stores only ciphertext. **Bank sync tokens are NOT E2EE** — they remain server-readable. ([docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/getting-started/sync/)) This is the **only app in the comparison set with real E2EE**, and it shows exactly the tradeoff Orange Way must solve: bank-sync credentials can't easily be E2EE because they need to be fetchable by the sync service.
- **Recovery.** Lose the E2EE password = lose the data. Actual does not document a recovery path; that's the honest zero-knowledge tradeoff.
- **Revocation.** Admin removes user; no documented key-rotation guidance post-removal.

### 2.9 Firefly III (open source)

- **Not really multi-user for households.** Each user gets a completely **isolated administration**. To share, docs explicitly say you "must share the username and password." ([docs](https://docs.firefly-iii.org/how-to/firefly-iii/features/multi-user/))
- **"Shared asset account"** is just a tag indicating both partners use it — not a technical share. Planned future feature: "multiple financial administrations" to allow one user to have personal + joint sets in one login. ([docs](https://docs.firefly-iii.org/explanation/financial-concepts/administrations/))
- Essentially, Firefly treats partner-sharing as a known gap.

### 2.10 Maybe Finance / Sure

- **Maybe Finance (maybe-finance/maybe)** was an AGPLv3 Rails personal finance app with a Family data model (default currency at Family level). **Archived 27 July 2025** with no plans to resume. ([GitHub](https://github.com/maybe-finance/maybe))
- **Sure** is the community fork. Still fundamentally single-user per Family, self-hosted, no E2EE beyond whatever the hoster configures. ([Sure repo](https://github.com/we-promise/sure))
- Neither ships zero-knowledge or advanced permission models.

## 3. Patterns worth adopting

1. **Per-budget (or per-scope) sharing owned by the budget creator** — the cleanest mental model. YNAB Together does this best: owner picks what each group member sees. Monarch's flat "everyone sees everything" is simpler to build but causes the most user complaints about privacy.
2. **Separate login per household member** — universal across Monarch, YNAB, Lunch Money, Rocket Money, Simplifi, Actual. Shared-login models (Copilot Magic Link) are the exception and feel dated. Orange Way should always have one account per human.
3. **Ownership attribution on accounts and transactions** — Monarch's "mine/theirs/ours" labeling, Lunch Money's "who added this" audit trail. Even without privacy, surfacing _who owns what_ is a massive UX win for couples who split bills.
4. **Free or cheap additional household members** — Monarch (free unlimited), YNAB (up to 6 included), Lunch Money (per-budget pricing). Anyone charging _per-seat_ for spouses is a loser category.
5. **Bank credentials flow per user** — every credible app connects bank accounts under the inviter's login and surfaces them into the shared view. This is the right pattern: a leaving partner reclaims their bank connections.
6. **A separate paid "Professionals" tier for advisors** — Monarch's $14.99/mo-per-client model is the only real advisor story in the consumer segment. That tier pays for itself and keeps advisors from polluting the household model.
7. **An explicit "hide this single transaction from my partner" toggle** — Monarch's eye icon is the only direct acknowledgement that couples need gift-privacy. It is imperfect (same mechanism as "hide from reports") but users love having it.
8. **Membership transfer, not deletion** — YNAB's "budget ownership can be transferred at any time" is a good model for when the primary wants to hand over the household to their partner.

## 4. Market gaps / opportunity (where Orange Way wins with ZKA)

1. **Real zero-knowledge for a household, not just a single user.** Actual Budget is the only app with E2EE, and it is single-tenant per budget. Nobody ships **role-scoped, per-member key wrapping** where partner A and partner B both hold independently revocable keys to the same encrypted household vault. This is exactly the cryptographic architecture Orange Way implements.
2. **Graceful revocation under E2EE.** Every competitor either (a) destroys the leaving user's data (Monarch removes their accounts, Rocket resets them) or (b) leaves them with full copies (YNAB, Actual). Nobody does **cryptographic revocation with re-keying of the remaining vault**. This is the divorce-safe story: partner leaves → their key is revoked → the shared vault is re-encrypted → their local caches are cryptographically dead.
3. **Per-row privacy that actually works under E2EE.** Monarch's eye icon is a UI filter, not a crypto boundary. Orange Way offers **per-transaction encryption** where a single transaction is wrapped only to the author's key, not the partner's. Partner literally cannot decrypt it.
4. **Read-only advisor role with scoped, revocable access.** Simplifi's top request, Empower's gap, Monarch's only via a separate $14.99/mo product. Orange Way ships a first-class "invite your CPA / tax pro" role for free, with a scoped, revocable, read-only wrapping of the vault key.
5. **Multi-role dependents.** Only YNAB has a real "teen has their own isolated budget inside the household" story, and only in Together. There is white space for: teen sub-budget that parents can fund (allowance), with a one-way visibility edge (parent sees child, child does not see parent), especially given the overlap with Bitcoin-native family-data themes Orange Way already plays in.
6. **Joint vs personal at one login.** Firefly III calls this out as the feature they don't have and plan to build ("multiple administrations per user"). Monarch, Copilot, Rocket, Simplifi all force one household per login. YNAB is the closest with per-budget sharing. Orange Way ships "joint vault + per-user vaults in one app" natively.
7. **Honest recovery under ZKA.** Actual silently leaves users stranded if they lose the E2EE password. Orange Way differentiates with an explicit, documented **social recovery** or **shard-based recovery** story (e.g. partner-assisted recovery, or offline recovery code printed at onboarding). This is a huge trust lever versus "we can reset your password, all your data is on our server."

## 5. Mistakes to avoid (from reviews and community complaints)

1. **Don't delete a leaving partner's data.** Monarch's "removing a member removes the accounts they added" is a documented footgun. A clean model keeps the leaving partner's account/transaction history in the shared vault (optionally anonymized or snapshotted), and only revokes their _access key_.
2. **Don't reset the leaving partner's app.** Rocket Money's "you will have to start fresh with a new Rocket Money account" after being unshared is widely complained about.
3. **Don't make "hide account" fake.** Monarch's hidden accounts still appear in Institution Settings and when hidden items are expanded. If you ship a hide control, the cryptographic guarantee must match the UI promise.
4. **Don't ship shared-login as "sharing."** Copilot Money's Magic Link means a single auth identity, so audit trails, revocation, and MFA are fundamentally broken. Every user must have a real account.
5. **Don't make joint-owner all-or-nothing.** Empower's inability to "limit a joint owner's access" and its one-way Joint Cash conversion are persistent complaint sources.
6. **Don't lock E2EE recovery behind "sorry, we can't help."** Actual's honest-but-brutal "we can't recover your password" is a trust tradeoff but also a churn driver. Document social recovery or escrowed recovery clearly from day 1.
7. **Don't charge per seat for spouses/partners.** Users perceive this as rent-seeking. Include at least 2 seats in the base plan.
8. **Don't ship "read-only sharing" as a promise you can't keep.** Simplifi's community has been asking for this for years; shipping a half-baked version damages trust. Do it right or don't ship it.
9. **Don't tie dependents to adult-only terms.** Monarch's 18+ terms of service mean there is no legitimate way for a teen to use the product — but the docs still say "parent, child" can be invited. Ambiguity is a compliance risk.
10. **Don't hide the audit trail.** Lunch Money's "who added this transaction" is a _positive_ competitive signal; partners fighting over budget changes need provenance.
